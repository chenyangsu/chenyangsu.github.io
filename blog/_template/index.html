The title of the post, written as a claim rather than a topic

Two sentences that say what happened and why it mattered. A reader who stops here should still leave with the point.

Chen-Yang Su · 30 August 2026 ·  

Open on the concrete thing that happened, not on background. The first paragraph should contain a number, a name, or a decision. Background, if it is needed at all, belongs in the second paragraph where the reader already has a reason to care.

Body text is Source Serif 4 at 18px. Links look like this. Bold marks the load-bearing phrase in a paragraph, and inline code such as PP.H4 ≥ 0.8 keeps thresholds unambiguous.

A section heading that advances the argument

Headings are Lato, so the article still reads as part of this site. Use them to mark turns in the argument, not to label topics.

A subheading, when a section genuinely has two parts

  • A list is for things that are genuinely parallel.
  • If the items are not parallel, they are paragraphs.
  • Three to six items. Beyond that, use a table.

A pull quote or a quotation from someone else. Attribute it in the sentence before, not in the quote.

What this does not show

State the limit of the specific claim just made. Naming it here is stronger than a hedge inside the sentence, and it keeps the argument in the main text clean.

Correction

The red variant is for corrections and warnings. If a published post turns out to be wrong, correct it in place with one of these, dated, rather than silently editing the text.

Figures

Put figures in /blog/POST-SLUG/ alongside the post, or in /images/blog/ if they are shared. Every figure needs a caption that says what is plotted, in what units, and on how many observations.
