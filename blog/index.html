    The hidden grammar of teamwork: Can AI learn how organizations work?

    Nine hospitals changed how doctors hand over patients at the end of a shift, and medical errors fell 23%. What changed was how information moved between people. Two Stanford researchers now propose training an AI on workplace events, the way language models train on words, to learn the patterns behind results like that one. So far it is a proposal, and it raises hard questions about prediction and privacy.

    When answers are cheap, what makes a person valuable?

    The end of intellectual boundaries? AI, epistemic trespassing, and the future of scientific discovery

    In October 2026, an AI agent solved 49 of 50 unpublished problems from 11 specialties of theoretical physics, after its objections sent the experts back to find errors in their own answers. Philosophers call it epistemic trespassing when an expert passes judgment outside their field. As AI makes it cheap to work in a field you never trained in, the ability to judge that work becomes the scarce part of science.

    Who will produce the knowledge AI depends on?

    About a thousand high-school students in Turkey practised maths with GPT-4. Their practice scores rose 48%, and then they scored 17% lower on the exam than classmates who had used only a textbook. Routine work used to make two things at once, the output someone paid for and the expert nobody paid for, and AI can now deliver the first without the second.
